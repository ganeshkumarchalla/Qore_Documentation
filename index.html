QORE DOCUMENTATION

Quantum computing, made tangible.

QORE is a local-first quantum computing workbench. It lets learners and teams write Qiskit circuits, execute them with Aer, explore visual state representations, inspect statistics, and keep reproducible records of each run.

This reference explains the concepts behind the interface and the practical workflow for building, simulating, validating, and sharing quantum experiments.

Quantum foundations

A qubit is a quantum information unit described by amplitudes. Before measurement, its state can be a superposition of |0⟩ and |1⟩. Measurement samples a classical bit using amplitude-squared probabilities. Multiple qubits form a joint state space, enabling interference and entanglement.

Superposition

H gates create a balanced state. The statevector holds amplitudes, while repeated shots turn those amplitudes into observable counts.

Entanglement

CX gates can correlate qubits. A Bell pair produces 00 and 11 together, even when each qubit alone appears random.

Circuits & gates

Quantum circuits apply gates in sequence. H, X, Y, Z, S, T, RX, RY, RZ, CX, CZ, SWAP, controlled gates, registers, measurements, and standard Qiskit circuit construction are supported through the local Qiskit SDK.

from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
qc = QuantumCircuit(2, 2)
qc.h(0)
qc.cx(0, 1)
qc.measure([0, 1], [0, 1])

Run this circuit with Aer to obtain a Bell-state distribution. With enough shots the only expected outputs are 00 and 11, subject to noise if a noise model is enabled.

Quantum Lab

Quantum Lab is QORE’s execution area. Start in Explorer, edit a circuit, then set shots, seed, optimization level, and noise options. The simulator returns counts, measured probabilities, ideal probabilities, TVD, timing, backend information, and warnings.

Use a fixed seed for repeatable sampling. Increase shots to reduce statistical variation. Check terminal output when a parse or runtime error occurs, and preserve the circuit and metadata when sharing a result.

State World & Bloch

State World gives a register-level view of basis states and measured outcomes. Select nodes to compare ideal probability, measured probability, and counts. Bloch views give a single-qubit geometric representation with axes, vector direction, purity, phase, and polar angle.

These visualizations explain a circuit from different perspectives. The distribution is best for measurements; the statevector and Bloch sphere are best for understanding the state before measurement.

Quantum Ledger

The Ledger is the evidence record for a run: source hash, backend, seed, shots, noise model, execution time, timestamp, warnings, TVD, framework versions, and historical snapshots. This makes experiments reproducible and easier to review.

Qiskit & Aer

QORE includes Qiskit, Qiskit Aer, the IBM Quantum Runtime client, and published add-ons for circuit cutting, approximate quantum compilation tensor methods, multi-product formulas, operator backpropagation, sample-based quantum diagonalization, and utilities. Aer executes supported circuits locally; IBM hardware requires secure server-side credentials.

Applications

Use QORE to explore quantum optimization, chemistry and materials simulation, cryptography, and hybrid machine learning. These are active research areas, not guarantees of speedup. Start by modeling a small circuit, establishing a classical baseline, and recording what the simulator demonstrates.

Deployment & support

For public code execution, isolate the backend, require authentication, rate limit requests, and enforce qubit, shot, memory, and execution-time limits. Never expose IBM service tokens to the frontend. For support, collect the project path, source code, simulator settings, terminal output, and execution metadata.
